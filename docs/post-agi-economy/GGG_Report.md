# Simulator Results: Interpretive Report

## 1. Introduction

This report interprets the numerical results generated by the four-domain governance simulator developed in the accompanying paper. The framework functions both as a constitutional theory of alignment and as a systemic model of how human–AI arrangements evolve under different coordination regimes.

The simulator models four coupled domains: Economy, Employment, Education, and Ecology. It tests the dynamic stability of the "Post-AGI" condition, defined not as a future technological singularity but as the current and ongoing integration of human and artificial agency. The framework is scale-free: the dynamics described here apply to national economies, institutional governance, and individual practice.

The analysis draws on data from seven distinct scenarios, a long-horizon stability test, and a historical calibration. It interprets alignment indices, aperture, displacement vectors, and stage profiles to determine how alignment goals (poverty resolution, meaningful employment, epistemic literacy, and ecological regeneration) are realised through operational alignment or obstructed.

## 2. Reading the Data

The simulator outputs specific metrics that quantify the structural health of the system.

**Alignment index:** A 0-100 measure of operational alignment.
*   **90 or above:** The domain is in the simulator's high-alignment region. Within the framework, that region marks the aperture condition associated with the four governance goals; empirical realization of those goals requires separate measurements.
*   **Below 90:** The domain remains outside that high-alignment region, with elevated coordination friction under the model metrics.
*   **2025 calibration endpoint:** The heuristic calibration assigns an alignment index of 17.2 to the 2025 endpoint.

**Aperture:** The balance between global coherence and local differentiation. The target is approximately **0.0207**.
*   **Aperture term of the governance potential:** Measures the deviation from this target. Low values indicate the system has found the correct balance between centralization and autonomy.

**Stage Profiles:** These reveal the internal configuration of each domain.
*   **Governance:** Capacity for traceable governance and decision-making.
*   **Information:** Capacity for informational variety and signal processing.
*   **Inference:** Capacity for accountable inference and conclusion.
*   **Intelligence:** Capacity for integrity and coherence of accountable information.

**Displacement:** Measures of accumulated structural failure recorded in the Ecology domain. Even when a system works efficiently (high alignment), it may generate displacement (debt).
*   **Governance Traceability Displacement:** Failures in governance traceability.
*   **Information Variety Displacement:** Failures in information variety.
*   **Inference Accountability Displacement:** Failures in inference accountability.
*   **Intelligence Integrity Displacement:** Failures in intelligence integrity.

## 3. Historical Context and Current Trajectory

The historical calibration places the present moment within a long-term trajectory of AI integration. The calibration assigns heuristic aperture values to key milestones:

*   **1956 (Dartmouth):** alignment 2.2
*   **1997 (Deep Blue):** alignment 3.0
*   **2016 (AlphaGo):** alignment 5.2
*   **2020 (GPT-3):** alignment 8.3
*   **2023 (LLM Adoption):** alignment 13.8
*   **2025 calibration endpoint:** alignment 17.2

**Interpretation:** The calibration assigns the 2025 endpoint an alignment index of 17.2 on the model's 0–100 scale. The rise from 5.2 (2016) to 17.2 (2025) indicates acceleration under the heuristic mapping, yet the gap to the 90+ threshold remains large and marks the modelled friction in the economy, where productivity gains are consumed by misalignment losses rather than generating distributable surplus.

Over the same period, global indicators of material progress have improved markedly. The share of the world population in extreme poverty has fallen below ten percent, child mortality has dropped by more than half since 1990, and average life expectancy has risen by several decades compared with the early twentieth century (Bregman, 2017; World Bank, 2024). Yet these gains have not resolved structural crises of poverty, unemployment, miseducation and ecological degradation. In the terms of this framework, productive capacity has increased while alignment remains low, so a large fraction of potential surplus is still dissipated through misalignment.

**Illustrative fitted crossing years:** Under the heuristic calibration and the stated mapping from simulation steps to calendar years, the estimated year for alignment 95 or above depends on coordination intensity:
*   **Weak Coordination (0.5):** approx. 2034.
*   **Canonical Coordination (1.0):** approx. 2028.
*   **Strong Coordination (2.0):** approx. 2025.

## 4. Scenario Analysis

The seven scenarios explore how different governance choices alter the path to alignment.

### 4.1 Scenario 1: Weak Coupling (Fragmented Governance)
*   **Parameters:** $\kappa=0.5$
*   **Final SI:** Economy 91.37, Employment 94.47, Education 95.71
*   **Displacement:** GTD 0.4167, IVD 0.2239, IAD 0.0462, IID 0.2987

**Analysis:**
This scenario represents a world where institutions improve locally but lack systemic coordination. The Economy (91.37) trails Education (95.71) and Employment (94.47).
*   **Stage Profile:** The Economy shows a high reliance on Information (Stg2 = 0.472) but very low Governance capacity (Stg1 = 0.088). This indicates an economy driven by market signals and data flows where governance and decision-making structures are weak.
*   **Outcome:** While SI > 90 is achieved in all domains by step 100, the Economy is the slowest to align. Under the framework's interpretation, surplus distribution remains constrained by the persistent gap in governance capacity.

### 4.2 Scenario 2: Canonical Coupling (Balanced Governance)
*   **Parameters:** $\kappa=1.0$
*   **Final SI:** Economy 99.29, Employment 98.66, Education 99.47
*   **Displacement:** GTD 0.4421, IVD 0.2181, IAD 0.0370, IID 0.3013

**Analysis:**
This is the reference trajectory. All domains converge smoothly to SI > 98.
*   **Convergence Order:** Employment aligns first (step 19), followed by Education (step 54), then Economy (step 67). This confirms that labour markets and work practices are the "fast layer" of governance adjustment, while economic structures possess more inertia.
*   **Displacement:** Even with near-perfect aperture alignment (V_apert = 0.000130), displacement remains non-zero. GTD is 0.4421. This reveals that efficiency in operational alignment (governance) does not eliminate structural costs; it merely manages them. High operational alignment coexists with ongoing governance-traceability maintenance costs.

### 4.3 Scenario 3: Strong Coupling (Centralised Coordination)
*   **Parameters:** $\kappa=2.0$
*   **Final SI:** Economy 99.39, Employment 99.55, Education 99.26
*   **Displacement:** GTD 0.4794 (Highest), IAD 0.0270 (Lowest)

**Analysis:**
This scenario simulates rapid, intense coordination.
*   **Stage Profile:** The Governance component (Stg1) drops to 0.027 across domains. The system relies almost entirely on Information (Stg2 ~ 0.44) and Inference (Stg3 ~ 0.28).
*   **Trade-off:** This is the fastest high-alignment regime, but it ends with the lowest Governance-stage values and the highest Governance Traceability Displacement. Under the framework's interpretation, that combination describes a brittle form of coordination: rapid cross-domain adjustment is achieved while the profile moves farther from the canonical Governance component. Strong coupling therefore improves aperture alignment without securing stage-profile alignment.

### 4.4 Scenario 4: Low Aperture Start (Rigid Governance)
*   **Parameters:** Initial aperture below the target
*   **Final alignment:** Employment drops to 85.84, Economy 93.86
*   **Displacement:** Intelligence Integrity Displacement 0.4830 (Highest), Governance Traceability Displacement 0.2042 (Lowest)

**Analysis:**
This scenario tests an over-rigid initial aperture. Authoritarian or heavily bureaucratic governance would be a possible institutional interpretation, not a variable directly represented in the simulator.
*   **Failure Mode:** While Economy and Education slowly improve, Employment aligns early (SI ~95 at step 60) and then collapses to 85.84. Under the framework's interpretation, the Employment decline indicates that an over-rigid initial configuration does not sustain the required differentiation of work patterns.
*   **Displacement:** The system achieves excellent traceability (GTD 0.2042) but ends with the highest Intelligence Integrity Displacement among the seven scenarios (IID 0.4830). This indicates a failure of coherence over time; the system is controlled but lacks intelligence integrity.

### 4.5 Scenario 5: Asymmetric Initial Conditions (Organic Growth)
*   **Parameters:** Education starts at SI 100, others low
*   **Final SI:** Economy 90.42, Employment 91.74, Education 92.84
*   **Structural Health:** V_stage 0.086 (Best), V_CGM 0.098 (Best)

**Analysis:**
This scenario simulates uneven development where one domain leads.
*   **Outcome:** The final SI scores are lower than in the Canonical scenario (low 90s vs 99s), but the internal structural balance (V_stage) is the best of all scenarios.
*   **Stage Profile:** Governance capacity (Stg1) is maintained at ~0.31, much higher than in the Canonical (0.06) or Strong (0.03) scenarios.
*   **Implication:** A slower, uneven path to alignment preserves more governance capacity and internal balance than a forced march. Under the framework's economic interpretation, SI > 90 supplies the governance conditions associated with surplus distribution, and this path reaches that region with better structural health.

### 4.6 Scenario 6: Equilibrium Test (Metric Imposition)
*   **Parameters:** Start at SI 100
*   **Trajectory:** Immediate collapse to SI ~40, recovery to ~93

**Analysis:**
This scenario shows that setting aperture to its target value does not establish equilibrium when the stage profile remains imbalanced. Setting the indicators to 100 without the underlying couplings produces an immediate drop in the three indirect-domain alignment indices. It eventually recovers to the same level as Scenario 5 (SI ~93), showing that the subsequent trajectory depends on the coupled update rules and stage profile rather than on the initial aperture value alone.

### 4.7 Scenario 7: Uniform Weights (Null Model)
*   **Parameters:** Uniform stage weights
*   **Final SI:** All > 98.8
*   **Displacement:** IAD 0.0107 (Lowest)

**Analysis:**
Convergence occurs even without stage-specific tuning from the Common Governance Model. This shows that the stage-specific CGM weights are not required for convergence under the current update rule. The weights change the distribution of displacement and the path of convergence, while the aperture dynamics continue to target A*. The null model therefore tests sensitivity to stage weighting, not the independent derivation of the target.

## 5. Long-Horizon Stability

The 1000-step test demonstrates that the high-alignment configuration is a stable attractor, not a transient state.
*   **Stability:** After step 200, SI values remain above 98.83 for the remaining 800 simulated steps.
*   **Precision:** Apertures stay within 0.0002 of the target.
*   **Conclusion:** Once the simulated domains enter the high-alignment region, no degradation is observed over the remaining 800-step horizon.

## 6. Macroeconomic and Ecological Interpretations

### 6.1 The Surplus Threshold
The framework treats usable surplus as a function of alignment because coordination failures consume resources that could otherwise be distributed. The simulator tracks the alignment side of this claim; it does not contain an explicit production or fiscal sector.
*   **Current State (SI 17.2):** Friction consumes potential surplus under the framework's interpretation.
*   **Threshold (SI > 90):** As achieved in Scenarios 1, 2, 3, 5, and 7, the framework classifies these regimes as structurally capable of reducing the coordination losses that obstruct surplus distribution. In the canonical scenario, Economy reaches an alignment index of 99.29. This is the strongest reported operational-alignment result for that scenario, not a direct monetary estimate of surplus.
*   **Implication:** Under the framework's economic interpretation, these regimes supply the governance conditions required for an Unconditional High Income to become structurally viable because the economy is no longer paying the "tax" of misalignment. As the programme evidence below indicates, smaller scale interventions that approximate this condition already exist; they demonstrate that when misalignment losses fall, redistributive schemes can be fiscally neutral or net saving at local scale.

### 6.2 Employment as a Fast Variable
The simulator consistently shows Employment aligning faster than Economy or Education. This validates the Gyroscope Protocol's positioning of work as the primary interface for alignment. However, Scenario 4 warns that if this alignment is forced through rigidity, it is unstable and will regress. Stable employment alignment requires the support of Education (capacity) and Economy (resources and allocation).

### 6.3 Ecology: The Displacement Ledger
Ecological SI remains near 100 in all scenarios because the universal-balance construction gives canonical memory a weight of 97.93 percent. The displacement vector carries most of the scenario-sensitive ecological information and varies substantially across the runs, with GTD ranging from 0.20 to 0.48.
*   **Strong Coupling:** Records the highest GTD at ecological closure (0.4794).
*   **Low Aperture:** Records the highest IID at ecological closure (0.4830).
*   **Asymmetric:** Distributes displacement evenly (~0.20-0.35).

Within the framework, these results represent environmental degradation as the downstream record of upstream governance choices. Testing that interpretation requires mapping the displacement components to observed ecological indicators. A "green" policy that ignores governance traceability in the economy (GTD) will fail to stop degradation, as the simulator shows these failures accumulate in the ecological domain regardless of the ecological SI score.

### 6.4 Programme-Level Evidence and Alignment Regimes

The simulator treats surplus and displacement structurally: when SI is low, coordination failures and externalised harms consume surplus; when SI approaches 90 or higher, those losses diminish and surplus becomes available for redistribution. Several well-studied programmes exhibit this pattern at local scale.

First, experiments with unconditional or weakly conditional income support show that such transfers tend to have small or positive effects on labour participation while improving health and educational outcomes and reducing net public expenditure. The Mincome guaranteed income trial in Dauphin, Canada, reported only modest reductions in paid work, concentrated among new mothers and students, but recorded a roughly 8.5 percent decline in hospitalisations and improvements in educational attainment (Forget, 2011, as summarised in Bregman, 2017). Negative income tax trials in the United States in the 1970s similarly found limited work reductions and significant gains in high school completion (Robins, 1985). More recent cash transfer programmes in East Africa, such as those studied by GiveDirectly, have found that recipients invest windfalls in assets, housing and small enterprises, with incomes and consumption increasing over the medium term and no increase in spending on alcohol or tobacco (Haushofer & Shapiro, 2016).

Second, Housing First programmes illustrate how resolving misalignment can reduce both human suffering and fiscal cost. Utah's long-running Housing First initiative offered unconditional housing and basic support to chronically homeless people. Over a decade, chronic homelessness in the state fell by approximately 74 percent, while estimated annual public expenditure per person fell from about $16,670 to $11,000 because emergency room visits, policing and court costs declined (Bregman, 2017, ch. 5; Tsemberis et al., 2004). Dutch city-level programmes using similar principles have reported benefit–cost ratios between 2:1 and 3:1 when reductions in criminal justice, emergency and health expenditures are included (Wolf et al., 2010). In the terms of this framework, these programmes reduce Governance Traceability and Inference Accountability displacement by restoring a coherent chain from resources to needs, so that a larger share of available resources reaches their intended purpose rather than being spent managing the downstream effects of misalignment.

Third, experiments in development economics indicate that simple interventions that directly reduce scarcity can have large effects on cognition and long-term outcomes. In a series of studies, Shafir and Mullainathan report that financial scarcity taxes cognitive bandwidth to a degree comparable with losing a night's sleep, impairing decision-making by the equivalent of roughly 13–14 IQ points (Mullainathan & Shafir, 2013). Randomised controlled trials in Kenya and India show that inexpensive health interventions, such as deworming treatments and provision of free insecticide-treated mosquito nets, substantially increase school attendance and reduce mortality; charging even small user fees sharply reduces uptake (Miguel & Kremer, 2004; Kremer & Miguel, 2007). These results are consistent with the view that Information Variety and Inference Accountability capacity is degraded under scarcity and restored when basic needs are directly met.

Collectively, these programme-level findings align with the simulator's core claim that many entrenched social problems become tractable when alignment improves. Unconditional income support and Housing First can be read as local increases in economic and employment alignment: they reduce displacement by making allocation chains more direct and intelligible. Bandwidth and health interventions can be read as local increases in educational alignment: they restore the capacities needed for Governance Traceability, Information Variety, Inference Accountability and Intelligence Integrity. The simulator's high-alignment regime, in which surplus distribution and regeneration become structurally viable, generalises these local patterns to the scale of coupled economy, employment, education and ecology.

## 7. Conclusion

The simulator produces a quantitative analysis of the framework's internal dynamics. It should be read alongside the TimesFM trajectory-forecasting result, which measures continuation accuracy and ranks scenarios by preference scores applied to forecast distributions.

1.  **Alignment is Accessible:** The path from SI 17.2 (2025 calibration endpoint) to SI > 90 is open. Convergence holds across 1000 random initializations and multiple coordination regimes.
2.  **Coordination Determines the Path:** We can choose the fast, brittle path of Strong Coupling (high GTD), the slow, fragmented path of Weak Coupling, or the balanced path of Canonical Coupling.
3.  **Structure Over Metrics:** Scenario 6 shows that targeting metrics alone fails; only structural reform (coupling and stage profile adjustment) creates sustainable alignment.
4.  **Scale-Free Applicability:** Since the dynamics arise from the geometry of the four principles, they apply equally to global macroeconomics and local organizational governance.

The data shows that the structural and operational alignment conditions for resolving poverty, unemployment, and ecological degradation are dynamically stable attractors. The transition to this regime is a function of coordination intensity ($\kappa$) and the maintenance of the four constitutive principles.

A separate forecasting experiment applies zero-shot TimesFM 3 to the first stretch of the simulator's own trajectories. In the seven-scenario forecastability run, TimesFM had the lowest mean absolute error in 80 of 105 scenario-channel combinations. Drift was better in 23, and persistence was better in 2. The 10% to 90% bands covered 90.5% of held-out continuation values overall. A second analysis scored each scenario's forecasted continuation against the alignment objectives of this paper and ranked the seven scenarios by that score: uniform stage weights and asymmetric initialization came out on top, weak coupling came out last, and the ranking matched the one obtained from the actual simulator continuations with a mean Spearman correlation of 0.74.
